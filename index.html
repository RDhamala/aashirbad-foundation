The Problem

Talent is everywhere. Access is not.

Young people in underserved communities like Dhading may face financial barriers that end a school year early, limited access to mentors and professional networks, and limited exposure to computers and modern learning tools. None of that reflects a student's ability. It reflects where they happened to grow up.

What we do

Three simple programs

Education support, mentorship, and digital opportunity keep students enrolled, guided, and connected to what's possible.

Education Support

We help students overcome financial barriers, like school fees, exam fees, and books, that can interrupt their education. Headteachers verify each recipient, and donors receive updates every term.

Learn more

Mentorship & Exposure

Weekly calls and career conversations with Nepali mentors help students see possibilities beyond their immediate surroundings, and stay on track with attendance and goals.

Learn more

Digital Opportunity

Shared computer access and guided digital learning give students meaningful exposure to technology, well before they'd otherwise encounter it.

Dhading · Education Support

Staying in school

A secondary-school student in Dhading was at risk of interrupting her studies because of education-related costs. After the need was verified through her school, Aashirbad helped cover essential expenses so she could continue the academic year. Her headteacher confirmed her enrollment the following term.

Why We Started

A blessing passed forward.

Aashirbad means "blessing." The foundation was created in memory of our founder's father, a schoolteacher who believed deeply in education. Our founder grew up in a remote village in Nepal without reliable electricity, internet, or nearby access to a computer, and later built a career in data and technology. That experience shaped a simple belief: young people with talent deserve access to education, guidance, and technology earlier in life.
