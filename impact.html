Impact & Transparency

What has happened since 2023, and how we account for it.

We started in Dhading with a simple question: if you cover the fees, pair a mentor, and open up access to technology, will students stay in school? Here is what we know so far, and how we steward every gift.

Results so far

Piloting since 2023. Updated annually.

100+ Students Supported

Tuition, hostel, exam fees, and books covered through needs-based support verified by headteachers in Dhading.

95% Retention

Of the students we support, 95% remain enrolled the following year. Headteachers confirm enrollment status each term.

40+ Mentors

Nepali volunteers check in weekly by phone to help students with studies, attendance, and planning for what comes after school.

10+ Classrooms Reached

Partner classrooms with structured digital access and guided learning, supported with setup and maintenance help.

How we measure

What we track and what we don’t know yet.

Each term, headteachers report which students are still enrolled and share exam pass rates. Mentors log weekly check-ins. We use this data to decide where to add support and where to adjust.

Retention: the percentage of supported students who remained enrolled into the following academic period.

  • Term-by-term enrollment verification from headteachers
  • Mentor check-in logs and student feedback
  • Digital opportunity participation tracked through school lab coordinators

We do not yet have graduation or employment data. Our oldest cohort is still in school. We will publish those numbers as they become available.

Where we work

Piloting in Dhading, building to scale.

We focus our resources in Dhading district, where our founder grew up and where we have strong relationships with schools, headteachers, and ward committees. By proving what works in one community, we're designing a model that can expand to similar rural districts across Nepal.

  • Partner schools verified through local ward committees
  • Mentors recruited from Nepali universities and tech professionals
  • Annual reviews to measure what's working before scaling

Why Dhading?

Dhading is a rural hill district where many families face financial barriers to schooling. Our founder's roots here give us the trust and local knowledge needed to make programs work.

Student stories

What our programs look like in practice.

Three examples of how education support, mentorship, and digital opportunity work day to day in Dhading.

Dhading · Education Support

Staying in school

A secondary-school student in Dhading was at risk of interrupting her studies because of education-related costs. After the need was verified through her school, Aashirbad helped cover essential expenses so she could continue the academic year. Her headteacher confirmed her enrollment the following term.

Dhading · Mentorship & Exposure

Seeing more options

For many students, the challenge is not only paying for school. It is also knowing what can come next. Through regular mentor conversations, students ask practical questions about higher education, different fields of work, and the steps needed to get there. Mentors check in weekly and help students set goals they can act on.

Dhading · Digital Opportunity

First time using a computer

At a partner school in Dhading, some students had very limited opportunities to use a computer outside the classroom. Shared devices and guided sessions gave them a chance to practice basic digital skills, explore educational content, and become more comfortable using technology. The goal is earlier exposure, not a device for each student.

How we work

From verified need to monitored support.

  1. A school or community identifies a student's or classroom's need.
  2. Aashirbad verifies the need with headteachers and ward committees.
  3. The board reviews and approves support, and funds are transferred to our program lead in Nepal.
  4. Goma coordinates with headteachers to pay fees, support digital opportunity programs, or fund mentor stipends.
  5. Progress is monitored each term, and donors receive an update showing where funds went.

An honest note

Four volunteers started this in 2023. We haven't been audited yet and our oldest scholars are still in school, so we don't have graduation numbers. As results come in, we will publish them here and in our annual reports. If you have questions about anything, including our finances, our programs, or our plans, please reach out directly.
