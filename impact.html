Impact & Transparency

What has happened since 2023 — and how we account for it.

We started in Dhading with a simple question: if you cover the fees, pair a mentor, and open up access to technology, will students stay in school? Here is what we know so far, and how we steward every gift.

Results so far

Piloting since 2023. Updated annually.

100+ Students Supported

Tuition, hostel, exam fees, and books covered through needs-based support verified by headteachers in Dhading.

95% Retention

Of the students we support, 95% remain enrolled the following year. Headteachers confirm enrollment status each term.

40+ Mentors

Nepali volunteers check in weekly by phone to help students with studies, attendance, and planning for what comes after school.

10+ Classrooms Reached

Partner classrooms with shared computer access and guided digital learning, supported with setup and maintenance help.

How we measure

What we track and what we don’t know yet.

Each term, headteachers report which students are still enrolled and share exam pass rates. Mentors log weekly check-ins. We use this data to decide where to add support and where to adjust.

Retention: the percentage of supported students who remained enrolled into the following academic period.

  • Term-by-term enrollment verification from headteachers
  • Mentor check-in logs and student feedback
  • Digital access participation tracked through school lab coordinators

We do not yet have graduation or employment data — our oldest cohort is still in school. We will publish those numbers as they become available.

Where we work

Piloting in Dhading, building to scale.

We focus our resources in Dhading district, where our founder grew up and where we have strong relationships with schools, headteachers, and ward committees. By proving what works in one community, we're designing a model that can expand to similar rural districts across Nepal.

  • Partner schools verified through local ward committees
  • Mentors recruited from Nepali universities and tech professionals
  • Annual reviews to measure what's working before scaling

Why Dhading?

Dhading is a rural hill district where many families face financial barriers to schooling. Our founder's roots here give us the trust and local knowledge needed to make programs work.

Dhading · Scholar

Sita stayed in school because fees were covered.

When Sita's family couldn't afford hostel fees mid-term, a scholarship kept her enrolled. Weekly mentor calls helped her stay focused through exams.

“When my hostel fee was covered, I could focus on finals instead of finding work. My mentor's weekly calls kept me on track.”
  • First in her family to finish secondary school
  • Now mentors younger students in her ward
Dhading · Mentorship

Bikash found direction through weekly calls.

Bikash was close to dropping out when a volunteer mentor began weekly check-ins. Career sessions helped him see a path forward in technology.

“I didn't know anyone who worked in tech. My mentor showed me it was possible for someone from my village.”

Dhading · Digital Access

Manju's school gained its first shared computer lab.

Manju's rural school had no computers until Aashirbad helped set up a shared lab. Students now get guided digital learning time during study periods and weekend labs.

“We used to only read about computers in textbooks. Now we can actually use them.”
  • School's first shared computer lab
  • Weekend coding sessions started for interested students
Dhading · Partner School

Principal Rai saw attendance improve.

After partnering with Aashirbad, Principal Rai's school saw fewer students dropping out mid-year. Scholarships, mentors, and shared digital access gave families a reason to keep children enrolled.

“Parents used to pull children out for farm work. Now they see the laptops and the mentors, and they want their children to stay.”
  • Fewer mid-year dropouts since the partnership began
  • Open classroom days invite nearby schools to visit

How we work

From verified need to monitored support.

  1. A school or community identifies a student's or classroom's need.
  2. Aashirbad verifies the need with headteachers and ward committees.
  3. The board reviews and approves support, and funds are transferred to our program lead in Nepal.
  4. Goma coordinates with headteachers to pay fees, support digital access, or fund mentor stipends.
  5. Progress is monitored each term, and donors receive an update showing where funds went.

An honest note

Four volunteers started this in 2023. We haven't been audited yet and our oldest scholars are still in school, so we don't have graduation numbers. As results come in, we will publish them here and in our annual reports. If you have questions about anything — our finances, our programs, our plans — please reach out directly.
